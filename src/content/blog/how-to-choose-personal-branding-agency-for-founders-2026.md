Choosing a personal branding agency is not the same as choosing a social media manager.

For a founder, the real job is bigger: the agency has to understand the business, clarify what the founder should be known for, turn that positioning into useful content, and build a repeatable system that can compound over time.

That means the best choice is rarely the team that promises the most posts or the fastest follower growth. It is the team that can connect **positioning, content, production, distribution and business context** into one system.

## Start with the business objective

Before comparing agencies, define what the founder brand is supposed to achieve.

Common objectives include:

- becoming more recognisable in a specific market
- building trust before sales calls
- attracting better-fit clients
- creating authority around a category or point of view
- supporting recruitment or partnerships
- strengthening the company brand through the founder's visibility
- creating a reusable content engine across LinkedIn, YouTube, Instagram or short-form platforms

If the objective is unclear, the agency will usually default to vanity metrics.

A useful first question is:

**"What should become easier for the business if the founder brand works?"**

The answer should influence the whole strategy.

## 1. Look for positioning depth, not just content production

A founder should not sound like every other person in the category.

Before content production starts, a strong agency should be able to help define:

- the founder's audience
- the category they want to own
- the problems they are credible to speak about
- the point of view that differentiates them
- the stories and experiences that create trust
- the topics that connect personal expertise to business demand

If the process begins with "How many posts do you want per week?", the strategy is probably too shallow.

## 2. Check whether the agency can preserve the founder's real voice

Founder content works best when it sounds like the person, not like a generic copywriter.

Ask how the agency captures voice.

Good systems may include:

- founder interviews
- voice notes
- recorded strategy calls
- opinion and story extraction
- examples of phrases the founder naturally uses
- a documented voice guide
- an approval loop that gets faster over time

The goal is not to make every sentence polished. It is to make the content recognisably yours.

## 3. Evaluate the content system, not individual posts

One strong post is easy to produce.

A strong system is harder.

A useful agency should be able to explain how one core idea becomes multiple useful assets without turning into repetitive content.

For example:

1. a founder records a 30-minute conversation
2. the strongest ideas are identified
3. one idea becomes a LinkedIn post
4. another becomes a short-form video
5. a deeper explanation becomes a YouTube segment or article
6. the team reviews performance and learns which themes deserve more attention

That is more valuable than simply filling a calendar.

## 4. Ask how the team uses platform differences

LinkedIn, YouTube, Instagram Reels and TikTok do not reward exactly the same behaviour.

A useful agency should understand the difference between:

- a LinkedIn text post that builds authority
- a short-form hook that must earn attention immediately
- a longer video that needs sustained retention
- a founder story that works as trust-building content
- a direct-response piece that should move someone toward an inquiry

Repurposing is useful, but copying the same asset everywhere is not a strategy.

## 5. Look for evidence that matches your goal

Case studies matter, but the metric should match the objective.

If your goal is authority and inbound demand, follower growth alone is not enough.

Useful evidence can include:

- stronger qualified inbound
- higher profile discovery
- increased branded search
- better engagement from the right audience
- more sales conversations
- stronger retention or watch time
- improved consistency
- clearer positioning
- better-performing content compared with the client's previous baseline

You should also ask what the agency *cannot* guarantee.

No credible team can guarantee virality, exact follower growth or a specific number of leads from an organic platform.

## 6. Understand who will actually do the work

Many agencies sell through a senior strategist and deliver through a completely different junior team.

Ask:

- Who owns strategy?
- Who writes?
- Who edits?
- Who reviews quality?
- Who communicates with the founder?
- How many clients does each strategist manage?
- What happens when priorities change quickly?

The delivery structure matters because founder content depends on context.

A team that knows the business can react faster and create better material than a disconnected production line.

## 7. Measure time required from the founder

The founder should be involved enough to provide expertise, stories and judgment — but not so involved that the agency creates another full-time job.

A good workflow should make founder input high-leverage.

The founder might provide:

- one strategy call
- periodic recording sessions
- voice notes
- fast approvals
- access to important business context

The agency should handle the repeatable execution around that input.

## 8. Ask how strategy changes after the first month

A personal brand should improve as the team learns.

The agency should be able to explain how it uses performance data to adjust:

- topics
- hooks
- formats
- posting cadence
- platform mix
- visual language
- calls to action
- content depth

If the content plan is fixed for six months regardless of results, that is a warning sign.

## 9. Check whether the agency understands business positioning

This is one of the biggest differences between a content vendor and a strategic partner.

Sometimes weak content performance is not really a content problem.

The underlying issue may be:

- unclear offer positioning
- weak differentiation
- the wrong audience
- inconsistent brand language
- a poor customer journey
- a lack of proof
- a founder talking about topics that do not connect to business demand

A strong team should be able to recognise when the content is only a symptom.

## 10. Compare agencies using one simple scorecard

Before deciding, rate each option from 1 to 5 on:

- positioning depth
- founder voice preservation
- content system quality
- platform expertise
- proof and case studies
- team quality
- communication
- business understanding
- reporting and iteration
- fit with your working style

This creates a much better decision than choosing based on follower count or a sales call.

## Questions to ask on the first call

Use these questions:

1. How do you decide what a founder should become known for?
2. How do you capture and preserve my voice?
3. What input do you need from me each month?
4. How do you decide which platforms matter?
5. What does your content review process look like?
6. Which metrics do you use to decide whether the strategy is working?
7. Can you show examples that are similar to my goals?
8. Who will actually work on my account?
9. How do you handle strategy changes when the business changes?
10. What outcomes do you refuse to guarantee?

The answers will tell you more than a polished proposal.

## The right agency should make your expertise easier to discover

A founder brand is not a collection of posts.

It is a system that helps the market understand:

- who you are
- what you know
- what you believe
- who you help
- why your perspective matters

When those signals become consistent, content starts doing more than generating impressions. It can support trust, demand, recruitment, partnerships and the company brand itself.
