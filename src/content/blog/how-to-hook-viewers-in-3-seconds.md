The first three seconds of a video decide almost everything. If a viewer doesn't feel a reason to stay, they're gone — and the algorithm reads that as a signal to stop showing your content. Here's the framework we use on every video we produce.

## Lead with the payoff, not the setup

Most creators warm up. They introduce themselves, explain what the video is about, and *then* get to the point. By that time, half the audience has already scrolled. Instead, open on the most interesting moment — the result, the surprise, the bold claim — and explain the context afterward.

## Create an open loop

A hook works when it opens a question the viewer needs answered. "I tripled this founder's reach in 30 days — here's the one change that did it" makes you want to know the change. The tension keeps them watching until you close the loop.

## Match the first frame to the promise

The opening visual should reinforce the hook, not distract from it. A clean, high-contrast frame with motion in the first second reads as "something is happening here." A static talking head reads as "safe to skip."

## Test, don't guess

We A/B test hooks constantly. The same video with a different first line can perform 3–5x better. Treat your first three seconds as the single most important edit in the entire piece — because it is.
